  en:
    background: 'OpenAI o1 showed that scaling inference-time chain-of-thought yields large reasoning gains, but its training recipe stayed closed and the open community was still relying on SFT over human-annotated CoT. The paper opens by noting that prior open explorations — process-based reward models, RL, and search algorithms such as MCTS and Beam Search — had not reached o1-level general reasoning. That left the source of the capability an open question: does it require supervised reasoning traces as a seed, or can it be incentivized directly?'
    problem: 'It was unresolved whether strong reasoning can be incentivized by pure RL on a base model — with no supervised reasoning demonstrations at all — and how to turn that into a usable general-purpose model. At bottom this is a question about where reasoning comes from: is human-annotated CoT a necessary seed, or will the right incentive alone make the model evolve its own strategies (self-evolution)? A practical gap rides along: raw pure-RL output is hard to read and mixes languages, so it cannot ship as-is.'
    prior_limits: 'Prior open attempts — process reward models, MCTS-style search, and large-scale human CoT annotation — ran into reward hacking, search-space explosion, and annotation cost, and none reached o1-level general reasoning. The paper''s own Unsuccessful Attempts section backs this with first-hand evidence: for PRM, fine-grained steps are hard to define and grade in general reasoning and a model-based PRM inevitably invites reward hacking, while for MCTS, token generation''s search space is exponentially larger than chess, so capping per-node expansion traps the search in local optima and training a fine-grained value model is inherently difficult. This failure list is precisely why the recipe collapses to outcome-verifiable rewards.'
    goal: 'Reproduce o1-class reasoning with an open recipe: prove the mechanism with pure-RL R1-Zero, make it usable via the multi-stage R1 pipeline, and transfer the capability to small dense models through distillation. The concrete deliverables are three: R1-Zero as an existence proof (RL applied directly to the base model with no SFT), R1 with cold-start data and multi-stage training for usability, and six 1.5B–70B dense models distilled from R1''s outputs. Releasing weights for all three tracks — so the recipe can be reproduced and extended outside closed labs — is an explicit goal.'
    method: 'Starting from DeepSeek-V3-Base (671B MoE, 37B activated), GRPO — a critic-free policy gradient that baselines each sample against its group''s mean reward — is trained with rule-based accuracy and think-tag format rewards to produce R1-Zero, and R1 adds a four-stage pipeline: long-CoT cold-start SFT on thousands of samples, reasoning-oriented RL with a language-consistency reward, rejection-sampling SFT on ~800k samples (600k reasoning + 200k non-reasoning), and a final all-scenario RL stage. GRPO drops the critic model (normally as large as the policy itself) and instead samples G outputs per question, scoring each against the group mean — grading sibling attempts at the same problem on a curve instead of keeping a separate judge model. Rewards are verified by rules — boxed final answers for math, compiler test cases for code — plus a format reward that forces the thinking into think tags; neural reward models were deliberately excluded because they suffer reward hacking at RL scale. The six distilled models get SFT only, on the same 800k samples, with no RL stage.'
    key_idea: 'Rule-verifiable rewards (answer correctness + output format) alone are sufficient training signal — self-verification, reflection, and long deliberation emerge from RL incentives without any neural reward model or reasoning SFT. The emblematic case is the "aha moment": an intermediate checkpoint interrupts itself ("Wait, wait.") to re-evaluate its initial approach, a behavior nobody taught it. It is like grading a student only on whether the final answer is right, never showing worked solutions — and watching them spontaneously develop the habit of checking their own work. Figure 3''s steadily growing response length is the quantitative trace: the model learns by itself to spend more test-time compute per problem.'
    validation: 'Evaluated on AIME 2024, MATH-500, CNMO 2024, GPQA Diamond, MMLU/-Redux/-Pro, Codeforces, LiveCodeBench, SWE-bench Verified, Aider, and open-ended judges (AlpacaEval 2.0, ArenaHard), with six distilled 1.5B–70B Qwen2.5/Llama3 checkpoints tested on the reasoning subset. The protocol caps generation at **32,768** tokens and reports pass@1 from temperature-0.6/top-p-0.95 sampling over 4–64 responses per question, since greedy decoding is unstable (repetition) for long-output reasoning models, and keeps prompts zero-shot because few-shot consistently degrades R1. The distilled six are checked on AIME 2024, MATH-500, GPQA Diamond, Codeforces, and LiveCodeBench.'
    results: 'R1-Zero lifts AIME 2024 pass@1 from **15.6% to 71.0%** (**86.7%** cons@64) by RL alone, and R1 reaches **79.8%** on AIME, **97.3%** on MATH-500, **71.5%** on GPQA Diamond, and a **2,029** Codeforces rating (96.3 percentile), on par with OpenAI-o1-1217. The gains generalize beyond math and code — **90.8%** MMLU, **84.0%** MMLU-Pro, an **87.6%** length-controlled win-rate on AlpacaEval 2.0 and **92.3%** on ArenaHard, all well above DeepSeek-V3. On the distillation side, the 7B distill (**55.5%** AIME) beats QwQ-32B-Preview, even the 1.5B distill outscores GPT-4o and Claude-3.5-Sonnet on math (**28.9%** AIME, **83.9%** MATH), and the 32B/70B distills exceed o1-mini on most benchmarks.'
    comparison: 'Benchmarked head-to-head against OpenAI-o1-1217, o1-mini, GPT-4o-0513, Claude-3.5-Sonnet-1022, DeepSeek-V3, and QwQ-32B-Preview; notably, distilled R1-Qwen-32B (**72.6%** AIME) far exceeds direct RL on the same 32B base (**47.0%**), showing distillation from a strong teacher beats small-scale RL. That contrast is close to a controlled experiment — **10K+ steps** of large-scale RL on Qwen-32B-Base with math/code/STEM data only reaches QwQ-32B-Preview level, while plain SFT on the teacher''s 800k samples adds another **+25.6pp**. Learning from a master''s worked solutions beats rediscovering everything alone at small scale — though the paper is explicit that pushing the frontier of intelligence still needs stronger base models and larger-scale RL.'
    significance: 'It open-sourced the first o1-class reasoning recipe, establishing verifiable-reward RL (RLVR) with GRPO as the default post-training paradigm and proving via 800k-sample distillation that small dense models can inherit reasoning. If o1 was a dish everyone could taste but nobody could cook, R1 is the paper that published the recipe — most open reasoning models since are variations on it (reviewer judgment). The documented negative results — why PRM and MCTS fail at RL scale — are a contribution of their own, steering the field away from expensive dead ends.'
    limitations: 'The paper states R1 trails DeepSeek-V3 on function calling, multi-turn, complex role-playing, and JSON output, mixes languages outside Chinese/English, and is consistently sensitive to few-shot prompting, so zero-shot use is recommended. Software engineering saw little gain over V3 because long evaluation times kept large-scale RL from being applied there. R1-Zero itself suffers poor readability and language mixing — the very reason the cold-start stage exists. And the thousands-of-tokens long-CoT decode is itself a real serving cost in latency and KV-cache pressure — like deliberating over the route while the taxi meter runs, the better answer comes with a longer fare (reviewer judgment).'
    future_work: 'Stated directions are leveraging long CoT for general capabilities (function calling, multi-turn, role-playing, JSON output), fixing multilingual mixing, and introducing rejection sampling or asynchronous evaluation to make software-engineering RL efficient. The authors also report that applying RL on top of the distilled models yields significant further gains, but deliberately leave that exploration to the community. The broader open problem is reward design beyond rule-verifiable domains — open-ended tasks that resist verification: a sprint is settled by a stopwatch but figure skating needs judges, and installing a judge (a learned reward model) reopens the door to reward hacking (reviewer judgment).'
    resources: 'Weights for R1-Zero, R1, and the six distilled models (based on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B/7B, Qwen2.5-14B/32B, Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct) are on HuggingFace, with the repository and weights under the MIT license. The distills additionally inherit their base-model licenses (Apache 2.0 for Qwen, the Llama community licenses). The RL training code and training data are not public, so full reproduction of the recipe is possible only at the level of the paper''s description.'

    DeepSeek-R1은 세 연구 계보가 만나는 지점에 서 있다. Alignment 계보는 InstructGPT(Ouyang et al., 2022)에서 시작해 RL을 LLM post-training의 표준으로 만들었지만, 모든 것을 human preference를 학습한 reward model 위에 세웠다. Verification 계보는 더 촘촘한 심판 쪽으로 밀고 갔다 — "Let's Verify Step by Step"(Lightman et al., 2023)은 모든 step을 채점하는 process reward model이 outcome-only 검증을 이긴다고 주장했고, 한동안 field도 동의했다. Test-time-compute 계보는 chain-of-thought prompting(Wei et al., 2022)과 STaR류 self-bootstrapping(Zelikman et al., 2022)을 거쳐 OpenAI o1에 이르러 더 긴 숙고가 정확도를 산다는 것을 입증했지만 recipe는 닫아뒀다. 정작 R1의 직계 조상은 사내에 있다: DeepSeekMath(Shao et al., 2024)가 이미 GRPO와 rule-checked math reward를 7B 규모에서 선보였다. R1은 그 기계장치를 671B base로 끌고 가서, 세 계보가 모두 맴돌던 질문을 정면으로 던진다 — supervised reasoning trace는 필수적인 씨앗인가, 아니면 비계(scaffolding)일 뿐인가?

    전환의 핵심은 이중 삭제다. RLHF의 왕관 보석은 reward model이었고 process-supervision 계보는 더 정밀한 심판을 원했는데, R1은 학습된 심판 둘을 모두 걷어낸다 — neural reward model은 대규모 RL에서 reward hacking을 부르기에 배제하고, critic은 GRPO의 group-relative baseline으로 대체한다. 남는 것은 조작할 수 없는 검증뿐이다: boxed 답이 맞거나 틀리거나, test가 통과하거나 아니거나. 더 깊은 역전은 reasoning이 무엇인가에 있다. 이전 recipe들은 chain-of-thought를 수집하고 복제할 데이터로 다뤘지만, R1-Zero는 incentive가 유용하게 만들 때 나타나는 행동으로 다룬다 — "aha moment"도 갈수록 길어지는 response도 누가 라벨링한 것이 아니라 선택된 것이다. Supervision의 밀도를 내주고 supervision의 비부패성(incorruptibility)을 얻는 거래이며, PRM·MCTS를 다룬 Unsuccessful Attempts 절이 더 촘촘한 대안들이 왜 무너졌는지를 기록해 뒀다.

    이 논문이 연 것은 이제 post-training의 기본 어휘다. Tülu 3(Lambert et al., 2024)가 verifiable reward 기반 RL에 RLVR이라는 이름을 막 붙인 참이었고, R1은 frontier 규모에서 그 existence proof가 되었으며, 몇 주 안에 open 재현 — Hugging Face의 Open-R1, 소형 base 위의 SimpleRL류 zero-RL 재현 — 이 뒤따랐다. Distillation 결과는 조용히 2층 경제를 만들었다: reasoning은 강한 base 위의 대규모 RL로 한 번 발견되고, 그 다음은 SFT로 값싸게 전파된다 — 같은 32B에 직접 RL을 돌리면 따라오지 못한다는 논문 자신의 비교가 그 증거다.

    풀리지 않은 긴장은 verifiability의 경계 그 자체다. 이 recipe는 정확히 스톱워치가 존재하는 곳 — boxed 답이 있는 수학, test suite가 있는 코드 — 에서 작동하고, 논문은 software engineering(느린 평가)과 open-ended task(검증할 rule의 부재)가 저항한다고 솔직하게 적는다. Field는 세 방향으로 밀 것이다: agentic·SWE task를 검증 가능하게 만드는 asynchronous·sandboxed evaluation, 학습된 판단을 다시 들여오되 그것이 되불러오는 reward hacking을 가두려는 rubric·generative verifier, 그리고 비용 길들이기 — 갈수록 길어지는 thinking trace는 이 recipe가 정확도에 지불하는 가격이기 때문이다. Verifiable frontier를 넓히는 쪽이 이 recipe를 새 도메인에서 돌릴 권리를 얻는다.

    DeepSeek-R1 stands where three research lines meet. The alignment line, from InstructGPT (Ouyang et al., 2022), made RL standard for LLM post-training but built everything on a reward model trained from human preference. The verification line pushed toward denser judgment — "Let's Verify Step by Step" (Lightman et al., 2023) argued that process reward models grading every step beat outcome-only checks. The test-time-compute line ran from chain-of-thought prompting (Wei et al., 2022) and STaR-style self-bootstrapping (Zelikman et al., 2022) to OpenAI's o1, which showed longer deliberation buys accuracy but kept the recipe closed. R1's direct ancestor is in-house: DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024) introduced GRPO and rule-checked math rewards at 7B scale. R1 carries that machinery to a 671B base and asks the question all three lines were circling — is the supervised reasoning trace a necessary seed, or just scaffolding?

    The shift is a double deletion. RLHF's crown jewel was the reward model, and the process-supervision line wanted an even finer judge; R1 removes both learned arbiters — the neural reward model because it invites reward hacking at RL scale, the critic replaced by GRPO's group-relative baseline. What remains are checks that cannot be gamed: the boxed answer matches or it doesn't, the tests pass or they don't. The deeper inversion is in what reasoning is. Earlier recipes treated chain-of-thought as data to collect and clone; R1-Zero treats it as behavior that emerges when incentive makes it useful — nobody labels the "aha moment" or the lengthening responses, they are selected for. Supervision density is traded for incorruptibility, and the Unsuccessful Attempts section records why the denser alternatives collapsed.

    What it opened is by now the default vocabulary of post-training. Tülu 3 (Lambert et al., 2024) had just coined RLVR for RL with verifiable rewards; R1 became its existence proof at frontier scale, and open reproductions — Hugging Face's Open-R1, SimpleRL-style zero-RL runs on smaller bases — followed within weeks. The distillation result quietly created a two-tier economy: reasoning is discovered once by large-scale RL on a strong base, then propagated cheaply by SFT — the paper's own 32B comparison is the receipt.

    The unresolved tension is the boundary of verifiability itself. The recipe works exactly where a stopwatch exists — math with a boxed answer, code with a test suite — and the paper is candid that software engineering (slow evaluation) and open-ended tasks (no rule to check) resist it. Expect three pushes next: asynchronous, sandboxed evaluation to make agentic and SWE tasks verifiable; rubric-based and generative verifiers that re-admit learned judgment while containing the reward hacking it brings back; and taming the cost of ever-longer thinking traces, the price the recipe pays for accuracy. Whoever widens the verifiable frontier gets to run this recipe on new domains.

## Notes

DeepSeek-R1 is the report that moved reasoning post-training from closed labs into the open: R1-Zero is the cleanest existence proof that rule-verifiable rewards alone can elicit long-horizon reasoning from a base model, and the four-stage R1 pipeline (cold-start SFT → RLVR → rejection-sampling SFT → alignment RL) became the template most open reasoning models now follow. The negative results are as instructive as the headline — neural PRMs collapsed to reward hacking and MCTS to search-space explosion, which is precisely why the recipe reduces to outcome-verifiable rewards. From an efficiency standpoint its two lasting artifacts are the distilled 1.5B–70B dense models, now a standard testbed for compression and serving work, and the shift of inference cost into long thinking traces — the very regime that makes long-generation KV-cache research matter. 이후 공개된 reasoning 모델 대부분이 이 recipe를 변형해 쓰고 있고, GRPO/RLVR 계열 후속 연구의 출발점이 된 논문이다.
