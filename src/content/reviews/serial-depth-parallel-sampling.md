  en:
    background: 'Masked diffusion language models (MDLMs) generate text by revealing masked positions over multiple rounds, choosing in each round which positions to fill based on model confidence in its own predictions -- a design that trades sequential (autoregressive) generation for parallelism, at the cost of some accuracy because tokens revealed in the same round are sampled independently even though the true distribution treats them as dependent.'
    problem: 'No existing theory pinned down the minimum number of rounds a value-adaptive policy needs to hit a given accuracy target for a fixed, known target distribution -- prior analyses either fixed the reveal schedule in advance (not adapting to what has already been generated) or bounded round complexity using entropy or negative log-likelihood, quantities this paper shows are fundamentally insufficient: two distributions with identical entropy can require a different number of rounds.'
    prior_limits: 'Entropy- and likelihood-based round bounds are properties of a specific decoder class (e.g. confidence-thresholded decoders), not of the target distribution itself -- a distribution can have high entropy yet fully independent coordinates that need only one round, so no entropy-based argument can certify how many rounds a general adaptive policy truly needs.'
    goal: 'The goal is to find the exact quantity that governs how many rounds of independent-within-round sampling are unavoidable for a given distribution and error budget, and to check what it implies for the confidence-based decoding rules real masked diffusion models actually deploy.'
    method: 'The authors define serial depth as the minimum expected number of reveal rounds needed to meet a KL-divergence accuracy target, then prove that this error exactly equals the expected conditional total correlation -- a classical multivariate-dependence quantity -- accumulated over the policy reveal rounds. They then derive round-complexity bounds for several distribution families (Markov chains, random walks, random permutations, structured binary strings) and separately measure realized costs on a small masked diffusion model (Qwen2.5-0.5B) converted to an MDLM and run on real WikiText text.'
    key_idea: 'The key idea is that total correlation -- not entropy, not likelihood -- is the exact currency of parallel-decoding cost: every bit of dependence a policy discards by sampling several positions independently in one round shows up, without approximation, as forward-KL error. This reframes the vague intuition of why parallel decoding loses accuracy into an exact accounting identity, the way a strict energy-conservation law lets a system behavior be predicted rather than only observed empirically.'
    validation: 'Beyond the exact theorems (proved for idealized distributions with known conditionals), the authors run a controlled empirical study on 542 real texts (WikiText plus model-generated code/math/prose/structured-data samples), comparing left-to-right, random, hierarchical-bisection, and greedy-confidence reveal schedules, and then measuring the schedules actually deployed by masked diffusion decoders (confidence top-k, global/block thresholding) under the identical protocol.'
    results: 'Left-to-right block decoding -- the standard semi-autoregressive mode most deployed dLLMs use -- is the worst reveal order, costing **2.7-29x** more than random order at matched round budgets. Greedy minimum-confidence selection, the exact heuristic underlying standard MDLM decoders, costs **1.4-13x more** than simple random-order reveals at equal parallelism, losing on every one of the 542 tested texts for the top-16 comparison. Threshold decoders achieve near-zero measured error only by secretly near-fully serializing -- at a 0.99 confidence threshold, on average only about one position clears the bar per round.'
    comparison: 'Against generic reference schedules at matched round counts, the deployed confidence-based rules are 3.9-11x more information-theoretically costly than a simple random-order or hierarchical schedule -- the diagnostic in this paper directly separates the information cost of parallelism (measured here) from the wall-clock speedups threshold decoders report elsewhere, which the authors attribute to caching and self-generation confidence dynamics rather than to genuinely resolving more dependence per round.'
    significance: 'For anyone building or evaluating masked-diffusion decoding schedules, this gives a decoder-independent diagnostic -- conditional total correlation, or its practically measurable pseudo-cost proxy -- that can audit a proposed decoding rule before deployment, rather than only benchmarking wall-clock speed after the fact; it also gives a principled explanation for why the field default confidence-ordering heuristic is a specifically costly choice, not just an empirically imperfect one.'
    limitations: 'The theorems apply to an idealized exact conditional-marginal oracle; the empirical measurements on a real MDLM are honestly flagged as order-dependent pseudo-costs rather than the total correlation of one coherent joint distribution, since a learned model conditionals need not be globally consistent. The empirical study also uses a single, small (0.5B-parameter) model and a single sampling temperature, which the authors explicitly call diagnostic rather than a universal benchmark.'
    future_work: 'The paper leaves as an explicit open theoretical question whether binary-alphabet serial depth can exceed the square-root exponent established here, up to the linear depth achieved by uniform random permutations.'

    둘째 문단 -- the shift. 이 논문은 정확히 그 빠진 조각을 제공한다 -- 휴리스틱이나 경계가 아니라, 병렬 디코딩 정책의 근사 오차를 하나의 고전적 정보이론 지표(조건부 총상관량)에 정확히 연결하는 항등식이다. 이는 질문을 어느 휴리스틱이 실전에서 가장 빨리 디코딩하는가에서, 고정된 목표 분포가 실제로 얼마나 많은 해소 가능한 의존성을 담고 있으며 주어진 디코딩 순서가 그것을 공짜로 버리고 있는가 아니면 어쩔 수 없이 비용을 치르고 있는가로 재구성한다. 실제 마스크 확산 디코더에 적용하면 이 항등식은 불편한 실증적 판정을 내린다: 이 분야의 기본 선택인 신뢰도 순서 리빌은 이 정확한 척도로 볼 때 최악에 가까운 순서이지, 그저 괜찮지만 불완전한 선택이 아니다.

    셋째 문단 -- what it opens. 가장 뚜렷하게 열려 있는 흐름은 이 이상화된 오라클 이론과 실제 모델 자신의, 전역적으로 일관되지 않을 수 있는 학습된 조건부 확률 사이의 간극을 메우는 것이다 -- 저자들도 바로 이 이유로 자신들의 실증 수치를 진정한 총상관량이 아니라 의사비용이라고 이미 표시해두었다. 만약 이 간극을(예컨대 의사비용을 모델의 보정(calibration) 품질과 연결하는 방식으로) 메울 수 있다면, 조건부 총상관량은 새로운 마스크 확산 디코딩 스케줄을 배포한 뒤 실시간 속도를 벤치마킹하는 것뿐 아니라 배포하기 전에 점검할 수 있는 실용적이고 디코더에 무관한 진단 도구가 될 수 있다.

    First paragraph -- lineage. Parallel decoding for masked diffusion language models has mostly been analyzed empirically -- confidence thresholds, entropy budgets, and various which-positions-to-reveal-next heuristics compared by measured accuracy and speed, with round-complexity theory (when it exists) typically bounding specific decoder classes via entropy or negative log-likelihood. This site has tracked the practical side of that line through reviews like [[flowblock]] (wavefront-parallel decoding schedules) and [[adalook]] (adaptive multi-step lookahead), both of which propose new decoding schedules without a distribution-level theory of what the irreducible cost of parallelism actually is.

    Second paragraph -- the shift. This paper supplies exactly that missing piece -- an exact identity, not a heuristic or a bound, connecting a parallel-decoding policy approximation error to a single classical information-theoretic quantity (conditional total correlation). That reframes the question from which heuristic decodes fastest in practice to how much resolvable dependence a fixed target distribution actually contains, and whether a given decoding order throws that dependence away for free or is forced to pay for it. Applied back to real masked-diffusion decoders, the identity delivers an uncomfortable empirical verdict: the field default choice, confidence-ordered revealing, is close to the worst possible ordering by this exact measure, not merely a decent-but-imperfect one.

    Third paragraph -- what it opens. The clearest open thread is bridging the gap between this idealized-oracle theory and a real model own, possibly incoherent, learned conditionals -- the authors already flag their empirical numbers as pseudo-costs rather than true total correlations for exactly this reason. If that gap can be closed (for example by relating pseudo-cost to a model calibration quality), conditional total correlation could become a practical, decoder-agnostic diagnostic to check before deploying any new masked-diffusion decoding schedule, rather than only after benchmarking its wall-clock speed.
