  en:
    background: 'Diffusion LLMs (LLaDA, Dream) promise non-autoregressive parallel generation, yet the measured inference speed of open models lags behind KV-cached autoregressive models. At the closed frontier the promise is already real — Mercury reports over **1,000 tokens/s** and Google DeepMind''s Gemini Diffusion over **1,400 tokens/s** — but open checkpoints fall short of even ordinary AR throughput. The paper traces this gap to two structural causes: bidirectional attention blocks KV caching, and multi-token parallel decoding degrades quality, so in the quality-preserving regime an open dLLM effectively commits one token per full-sequence forward pass.'
    problem: 'Bidirectional attention rules out the standard AR KV cache, and naively committing many tokens per step sharply degrades generation quality. Without a cache, every denoising step recomputes attention over the entire sequence, so an architecture built for parallelism ends up slower than the AR models it competes with. The quality side is what the paper calls the "curse of parallel decoding": simultaneously committed tokens are drawn from independent marginals that ignore joint dependencies — like several writers each filling in one blank of the same sentence without seeing each other''s choices, every word plausible alone but the sentence incoherent jointly.'
    prior_limits: 'Prior dLLM inference recomputed full attention at every denoising step, and fixed token-per-step schedules break inter-token dependencies under the conditional independence assumption — identified here as the root cause of parallel-decoding quality loss. LLaDA''s default top-K scheme commits K tokens per step whether the model is confident or not, which is why accuracy falls as K grows (the top-2/4/8 baselines in Figure 5). Neither bottleneck had a training-free fix: caching was considered incompatible with bidirectional attention, and parallelism existed only as a fixed, non-adaptive schedule.'
    goal: 'Bring dLLM inference throughput close to AR levels training-free, without touching model weights and with minimal accuracy loss. Both bottlenecks must fall together — reusing computation across steps (caching) and cutting the number of steps (parallel commits) — because fixing only one leaves the other dominating runtime, as the ablation later confirms (**3.2×** and **2.5×** alone vs **8.1×** combined). The constraint is strict drop-in deployment: existing LLaDA/Dream checkpoints, with changes confined to the decoding loop.'
    method: 'Generation proceeds block by block with prefix KV activations cached and refreshed only at block boundaries (the DualCache variant also caches the masked suffix), combined with confidence-aware parallel decoding that commits only tokens whose max-softmax confidence exceeds a threshold τ (default **0.9**), with a highest-confidence fallback to guarantee progress. After a block completes, the caches of all blocks are recomputed once, so staleness is bounded by the block size (default **32**, swept 4-32; threshold swept 0.5-1.0). The cache treats the rest of the sequence like the frozen background of a film set: only the active block is live, and the background is re-shot once per block rather than once per frame. An appendix variant, factor decoding, adaptively picks the largest n satisfying (n+1)(1−c⁽ⁿ⁾) < f, tying the parallelism level directly to the theorem''s bound below.'
    key_idea: 'KV activations outside the active block are nearly static across adjacent denoising steps — the cosine-similarity heatmaps of Figure 3 concentrate near the diagonal for both prompt and suffix blocks — so approximate reuse is safe. Theorem 1 then proves greedy parallel decoding exactly matches greedy sequential decoding whenever each of the n committed tokens carries confidence above 1−1/(n+1), i.e. (n+1)·ε ≤ 1, and shows this bound is tight. The bar rises with the crowd: committing more tokens at once demands that each be closer to certain — like letting several people speak simultaneously only when each is reciting a foregone conclusion. A second clause bounds the distributional distance between the true joint and the factorized product, covering the non-greedy case as well.'
    validation: 'Evaluated LLaDA/LLaDA-1.5/Dream (plus multimodal LLaDA-V) on GSM8K (5-shot), MATH (4-shot), HumanEval (0-shot), MBPP (3-shot) (+MathVista/MathVerse), generation lengths 256-1024, single A100 80GB, using the standardized lm-eval library. Throughput is measured end-to-end as average output tokens per second until <eos>, at batch size 1 with no serving frameworks — the numbers reflect raw decoding-loop gains. Component-wise results (+Cache, +Parallel, +both) are reported separately across every benchmark and length, isolating the two contributions rather than showing only the combined figure.'
    results: 'Up to **27.6×** end-to-end throughput (LLaDA, 8-shot prefill, 1024 tokens) and **11.0×** on GSM8K-512, with accuracy within 1-2 points (GSM8K-256: 79.3 → **78.5%**); component-wise, KV cache alone gives **3.2×**, parallel decoding **2.5×**, combined **8.1×** (6.7 → 54.4 tokens/s). Gains grow with prefill and generation length — exactly where the recompute burden is heaviest — with DualCache benefiting most. On multimodal LLaDA-V, a refresh-based full-block variant reaches **9.9×** on MathVista with MathVerse accuracy even slightly improved (28.5 → **28.6**), and the appendix factor-decoding variant pushes GSM8K-256 to **11.7×** at 77.5% accuracy.'
    comparison: 'Compared against vanilla LLaDA/Dream decoding and fixed top-K token-per-step baselines, showing the two components compose multiplicatively (3.2× · 2.5× → 8.1×). Figure 5 shows the confidence threshold dominating fixed top-2/4/8 schedules: adaptive parallelism commits many tokens on easy steps and throttles on hard ones, holding accuracy where fixed-K degrades. Appendix C.5 adds an honest cross-family comparison against AR LLaMA under varying batch sizes: comparable or better at small batch, overtaken as batch grows.'
    significance: 'The first widely adopted recipe that makes open dLLM inference practically competitive with KV-cached AR decoding without retraining, formalizing cache staleness and adaptive parallelism as measurable tuning knobs and becoming the standard baseline for subsequent dLLM acceleration work (reviewer judgment). The deeper implication is that dLLM slowness was a decoding-loop property, not a weights property: two interventions that never touch the model recover an order of magnitude. Open dLLMs were not slow because the engine was weak — they shipped with two handbrakes on (full recompute every step, one token per step), and this paper releases both.'
    limitations: 'The paper itself notes that at larger batch sizes full-attention overhead lets AR LLaMA overtake it in throughput, and that multimodal LLaDA-V is block-size sensitive (**>8%** accuracy drop shrinking blocks 96→8 on MathVista); a systematic accuracy-staleness frontier map remains open (reviewer judgment). The batch mechanism is instructive: as batch grows, AR decoding transitions from memory-bound to compute-bound and recovers GPU utilization, while the dLLM keeps paying full attention over the whole sequence every step — the headline wins are fundamentally small-batch results. Theorem 1''s equivalence clause also covers only greedy decoding; sampling regimes get distance bounds, not equivalence.'
    future_work: 'No formal future-work section; natural next steps are per-position adaptive thresholds, staleness-aware cache refresh, and composing with AR-style cache compression (quantization/eviction) (reviewer judgment). The batch-scaling gap the authors acknowledge points at serving-level integration — batching strategies and cheaper attention for dLLMs — as the binding follow-up problem (reviewer judgment). The refresh-interval technique used for LLaDA-V (fixed block plus periodic cache refresh) also remains an unexplored scheduling knob awaiting systematic study.'
    resources: 'Code and project page are released under NVlabs on GitHub (Fast-dLLM); being training-free, no separate checkpoints are required. The method runs directly on public LLaDA, LLaDA-1.5, Dream, and LLaDA-V checkpoints, and evaluation goes through the standardized lm-eval library, so the headline numbers are reproducible end to end with public tooling.'

## Notes

Fast-dLLM is the paper that made open diffusion-LLM inference feel competitive: it identifies the two structural reasons LLaDA and Dream decode slowly — no KV cache under bidirectional attention, and dependency violations under naive parallel commits — and fixes both without any retraining. The block-wise approximate cache turns "how stale can cached states get?" into a tunable block-size knob, while the (n+1)·ε ≤ 1 condition of Theorem 1 gives the confidence-threshold heuristic an actual correctness footing rather than leaving it purely empirical. Its main honestly-stated boundary is batching: at larger batch sizes the full-attention compute floor lets autoregressive models pull back ahead, which is exactly where follow-up work on dLLM serving now concentrates.

LLaDA 계열 dLLM 가속 논문을 읽을 때 사실상의 공통 베이스라인이므로, block size와 confidence threshold 두 knob의 trade-off 곡선을 기준점으로 잡고 후속 논문들을 비교하면 좋다.
