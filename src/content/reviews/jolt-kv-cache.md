    method: 'Mistral-7B 15번째 층의 캐시를 세 방식으로 unfold해 특이값을 측정한 결과, 헤드 축은 거의 비압축(특이값 비 ~1.4-1.5)인 반면 토큰·특성 축은 실질적으로 감쇠하고, 그 안에서도 key는 특성 축이 날카롭게(비 27.4) value는 완만하게(비 2.7) 압축 가능하다는 비대칭이 드러난다. 이 측정에 따라 JoLT는 헤드·층 축은 항등으로 고정한 채 토큰·특성 축만 partial Tucker 분해로 절단하고, 절단이 버린 에너지를 Johnson-Lindenstrauss 회전 후 저비트(0/2/4/8비트)로 양자화한 잔차로 복원하며, 하나의 Lagrangian dual이 층 그룹별·K/V별로 Tucker 랭크와 잔차 비트폭을 하나의 바이트 예산 아래 함께 결정한다.'

    comparison: '동일 압축 예산에서 4비트 양자화·층 간 SVD(xKV) 대비 재구성 오차가 한 자릿수 낮고, CP·t-SVD·tensor-train 같은 다른 다중선형 분해 방식 중에서도 Tucker가 모든 압축률에서 최선임을 직접 비교로 확인한다(최선의 대안조차 2배에서 value 오차가 약 0.28로 JoLT의 0.006보다 훨씬 나쁨).     significance: '긴 문맥·큰 배치에서 서빙 처리량의 상한을 정하는 것이 모델 가중치가 아니라 KV 캐시인 상황에서, 캐시의 세 축이 서로 다른 정도로 압축 가능하다는 이 스펙트럴 측정은 캐시 압축을 설계할 때 첫 질문이 되어야 할 것을 보여준다: 어느 축이 실제로 중복되어 있는가. 다만 이 저장 공간 절감이 곧바로 디코딩 지연 절감으로 이어지지는 않는다는 점도 함께 드러나는데, 이는 압축의 실제 배포 가치를 가늠할 때 중요한 구분이다.'

  en:
    background: 'Transformer inference reuses every previous token''s key and value projections at each layer, so implementations cache them rather than recompute — a cache that grows with batch size, context length, and depth, and that becomes the binding constraint on memory and throughput before the model weights do once context or batch size grows large. Existing compression methods split into two families: low-rank methods that factor two-dimensional slices of the cache (Palu factors per-head matrices, xKV factors cross-layer feature blocks) and quantization methods that lower the bit-width of every entry (KIVI, TurboQuant).'
    problem: 'Neither family exploits the fact that the cache at a layer is naturally a third-order tensor -- heads, tokens, and features -- whose three axes carry very different amounts of redundancy. A two-dimensional factorization must commit to one axis of redundancy and ignore the others, and a fixed-bit-width quantizer cannot reach the modest, intermediate compression ratios (like 2-3x) where quality is still free, because even its smallest bit setting already overshoots them.'
    prior_limits: 'Low-rank methods like Palu and xKV treat the cache as a collection of matrices rather than a single object seen across heads, tokens, and features at once, and provide no residual term to restore the energy a truncation discards. Fixed-bit-width quantizers like KIVI and TurboQuant are strong at their native bit-rates but structurally cannot express compression ratios below their floor -- a 4-bit scheme, for instance, cannot reach below roughly 4x.'
    goal: 'Measure where redundancy in the KV cache actually lives across its three axes, and use that measurement to design a compressor that leaves the head and layer axes intact, compresses only the token and feature axes, and budgets keys and values separately -- reaching a near-lossless result in the 2-3x band.'
    method: 'Taking the singular values of each 1D unfolding of a real KV cache (Mistral-7B, layer 15) shows the head axis is essentially incompressible (singular-value ratio ~1.4-1.5), while the token and feature axes decay substantially -- and within those, keys compress sharply on the feature axis (ratio 27.4) while values compress only mildly (ratio 2.7), an asymmetry driven by projection geometry. Following this measurement, JoLT pins the head and layer axes as identity, truncates only the token and feature axes via a partial Tucker decomposition, restores the discarded truncation energy with a Johnson-Lindenstrauss-rotated low-bit residual (0/2/4/8 bits), and solves a single Lagrangian dual that jointly allocates Tucker ranks and residual bit-width, per layer group and separately for keys and values, under one shared byte budget.'
    key_idea: 'The central move is putting rank and residual bits on the same error scale so one optimizer can shift budget freely between them, and between keys and values, rather than fixing that split in advance -- like routing water from a single pipe to wherever it is needed instead of filling two separate reservoirs (rank, residual bits) independently. Because values must spread energy across directions to preserve content (a flat spectrum) and are therefore 2-3x harder to compress than keys at every ratio, this freedom to move budget across the asymmetry is what a fixed split could never exploit.'
    validation: 'Evaluated on Mistral-7B-v0.3 (GQA) and LLaMA-2-13B (MHA), using WikiText-2+C4 perplexity, 8-shot chain-of-thought GSM8K, and RULER single-needle retrieval out to 16K context, with perplexity averaged over seeds {0,1,2} whose standard deviation stays at or below 0.009 PPL in every cell. Ablations removing the residual, swapping the joint dual for a greedy allocator, or using uniform instead of per-group rank each independently confirm that design choice matters.'
    results: 'In the 2-3x band, perplexity, GSM8K accuracy, and RULER retrieval all sit within statistical noise of the uncompressed baseline -- at 2x, relative Frobenius error is 0.009 (K) and 0.006 (V), roughly an order of magnitude below 4-bit quantization (0.08/0.13) or cross-layer SVD (0.08/0.24) at the same budget. Beyond that free zone the two architectures split sharply: the GQA model (Mistral) degrades gradually, while the MHA model (LLaMA) falls off a cliff between 4x and 5x, with GSM8K collapsing from 28.13% baseline to 18.5%, 6.0%, and 1.5% at 4x, 6x, and 8x.'
    comparison: 'At matched compression budget, reconstruction error is an order of magnitude below both 4-bit quantization and cross-layer SVD (xKV), and a direct multilinear-format comparison shows Tucker beats CP, t-SVD, and tensor-train at every ratio on both architectures (the best alternative format still reaches only ~0.28 value error at 2x, versus JoLT''s 0.006). Unlike Palu or xKV, JoLT deliberately leaves the head and layer axes uncompressed -- a choice validated by giving a full four-mode allocator complete freedom and observing it drives head and layer ranks back to full size on its own.'
    significance: 'In a regime where the KV cache, not the model weights, sets the throughput ceiling at long context or large batch size, this spectral measurement of uneven axis-wise compressibility reframes the first question any cache-compression design should ask: which axis actually holds the redundancy. It also surfaces an important distinction for judging real deployment value -- a storage saving here does not yet automatically translate into a decoding-latency saving.'
    limitations: 'The authors state the most important limitation themselves: decoding must reconstruct the cache from its Tucker factors at every step, and "today''s attention kernels are not built to absorb" that cost -- so the storage win is not yet a deployable inference-latency path. Validation is confined to a single A100 GPU, two models (7B/13B scale), and contexts up to 16K, and the MHA architecture falls off sharply past 4x, so no single compression ratio is safe across architectures.'
    future_work: 'The authors explicitly name a fused kernel -- one that computes attention directly from the encoded factors without materializing the reconstructed cache -- as the key remaining systems direction, framing it as the gap between today''s storage win and a deployable latency win. Calibration robustness across text domains is stated as "in progress," and the long-context cap policy is validated only to 8192 tokens.'

    KV 캐시 압축은 오랫동안 두 갈래로 나뉘어 발전해 왔다. 저랭크 계열은 Palu(헤드별 SVD)에서 xKV(층 간 정렬된 특이벡터 추출)로 이어지며 캐시를 2차원 슬라이스의 모음으로 보고 그 안의 저랭크 구조를 짜낸다. 양자화 계열은 KIVI의 튜닝-불필요 비대칭 2비트 양자화, TurboQuant의 무보정 온라인 벡터 양자화로 이어지며 모든 항목의 비트폭을 낮추는 쪽을 택한다. 두 계열 모두 나름의 방식으로 성공했지만, 캐시를 헤드·토큰·특성이라는 세 축을 가진 하나의 3차 텐서로 정면으로 바라본 적은 없었다.

    이 논문의 전환은 그 텐서를 있는 그대로 측정하는 데서 시작한다는 점이다 — 세 축 각각을 unfold해 특이값을 직접 재고, 헤드 축은 버릴 것이 없고 토큰·특성 축에만 중복성이 있으며 그 안에서도 key와 value가 정반대 방향으로 압축 가능하다는 사실을 먼저 확인한 뒤에야 압축기를 설계한다. 그 설계 자체도 두 계열을 대립시키지 않고 결합한다: partial Tucker 분해(저랭크 계열의 도구)로 큰 구조를 잡고, 남은 것은 회전된 저비트 잔차(양자화 계열의 도구)로 채우며, 이 둘의 예산을 하나의 Lagrangian dual이 키와 값 사이에서까지 자유롭게 재분배한다. 각 설계 선택이 소거 실험으로 뒷받침된다는 점 — 특히 4축 전체에 자유를 줘도 최적화기가 스스로 헤드·층 축을 포기하지 않는다는 확인 — 은 이 결합이 임의적 절충이 아니라 측정에서 도출된 결과임을 보여준다.

    이 논문이 스스로 열어두는 다음 단계는 두 가지다. 하나는 저자들이 명시적으로 지목하는 시스템 과제로, 매 디코딩 단계마다 Tucker 인자에서 캐시를 다시 조립해야 하는 지금의 방식을 대체할, 인자에서 직접 attention을 계산하는 융합 커널이다 — 이것이 채워지기 전까지는 저장 공간 절감이 지연시간 절감으로 자동 전환되지 않는다. 다른 하나는 GQA와 MHA 사이에서 관찰된 뚜렷한 차이(GQA는 완만히, MHA는 절벽처럼 무너짐)를 아키텍처 특성으로부터 설명하는 일로, 논문은 이를 경험적 사실로 보고할 뿐 왜 그런지는 열어둔다 — 이는 압축이 "얼마나"뿐 아니라 "어떤 아키텍처에서"라는 질문도 함께 다뤄야 함을 시사한다.

    KV-cache compression has long developed along two separate lines. The low-rank line runs from Palu (per-head SVD) to xKV (cross-layer aligned singular-vector extraction), treating the cache as a collection of 2D slices and squeezing out the low-rank structure within each. The quantization line runs from KIVI's tuning-free asymmetric 2-bit scheme to TurboQuant's calibration-free online vector quantizer, choosing instead to lower every entry's bit-width uniformly. Both lines have succeeded on their own terms, but neither had looked the cache in the eye as a single third-order tensor spanning heads, tokens, and features.

    This paper's shift is to start by measuring that tensor directly -- unfolding each of its three axes and taking real singular values, establishing first that the head axis has nothing to give up while the token and feature axes hold real redundancy, and that keys and values are compressible in opposite directions within those axes -- and only then designing the compressor. The design itself refuses to pick a side between the two prior lines: a partial Tucker decomposition (the low-rank family's tool) captures the coarse structure, a rotated low-bit residual (the quantization family's tool) restores what truncation discards, and a single Lagrangian dual redistributes the combined budget freely, even between keys and values. That every design choice survives an ablation -- especially the check that a fully free four-mode allocator voluntarily gives back the head and layer axes on its own -- signals this combination was measured into existence, not stitched together as an arbitrary compromise.

    The paper leaves two things open by its own account. One is the systems problem it names explicitly: today's design must reassemble the cache from its Tucker factors at every decoding step, and a fused kernel that computes attention directly from those factors is the missing piece before a storage win becomes a latency win. The other is the sharp GQA-versus-MHA split it observes (graceful degradation versus a cliff) without explaining from architecture first principles why it happens -- reported honestly as an empirical fact rather than a derived one, which suggests future cache-compression work needs to ask not just how much to compress, but on which architecture that budget is safe to spend.

## Notes

The paper is unusually careful about what "near-lossless" does and does not mean here: reconstruction error at 2x is small but explicitly nonzero everywhere (0.009/0.006 relative Frobenius error), and the free-zone claim rests on task-level metrics (perplexity, GSM8K, RULER) staying within statistical noise rather than on the reconstruction being exact. The clearest open thread is the gap between a storage result and a latency result -- the abstract's framing ("shrinking the cache... is therefore a direct lever on throughput") is easy to read as already covering decode-time cost, but the paper's own conclusion is explicit that the fused kernel needed to realize that latency win is future work, not a delivered result.
