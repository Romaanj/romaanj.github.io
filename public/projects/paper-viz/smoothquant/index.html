SmoothQuant, Reproduced

Activations will not quantize because a few channels are tens of times louder than the rest, and one scale for the whole tensor leaves everyone else with a handful of levels. The fix is not a better quantizer — it is the observation that Wx does not change if you divide one operand and multiply the other. Every measured number here comes from Qwen3-8B, on CPU.

Q1 · Background

The hardware already has the units

An A100 does INT8 matrix math at twice the rate of FP16 and reads half the bytes to do it, so a lossless INT8 path for both operands would roughly double serving throughput for free — the arithmetic units are already sitting there.

Weight-only quantization (GPTQ, the previous page in this track) shrinks the bytes but still computes in FP16: the integers are unpacked back to floats before the matmul. W8A8 is the other prize — quantize the activations too, and the multiply itself runs in INT8. The catch is in the activations, and it is the exact tensor the foundation page raised into three axes.

Q2 · Problem

A typical channel gets nine of its 256 levels

Under one scale for the whole tensor, a channel whose maximum is m_i inside a tensor whose maximum is m receives only 2⁸·m_i/m effective levels — and because a few channels are tens of times louder than the rest, the ordinary ones are left quantizing into single digits.

This is the sharpest sentence in the paper (§3), and it is not a benchmark — it is arithmetic you can run on any tensor. Below it is run on a real Qwen3-8B layer, over a real calibration set. INT8 promises 256 levels; the median channel is actually working with about nine of them.

Q3 · Existing limitations

The obvious fix is unexpressible, not slow

A scale per input channel would fix the tax outright — the paper measures it, and it recovers full accuracy — but an INT8 GEMM can only apply scales along the outer dimensions, because a per-input-channel scale would have to be applied inside the reduction, between Tensor Core instructions.

So the field went sideways instead. LLM.int8() keeps the outlier channels in FP16 and runs a second, mixed-precision matmul for them. ZeroQuant quantizes per token, which is an outer dimension and therefore legal — but the variance that hurts is across channels within a token, so per-token barely helps. Three granularities, and the one the kernel cannot run

Left: relative output error of the layer under each scheme, ours. Right: what the paper measured end to end, zero-shot accuracy on OPT.

Per-token is on an outer dimension and legal; it lands nearly on top of per-tensor. Per-channel is a different order of magnitude — and it is the one that cannot be expressed inside the reduction. That gap, between what works and what the kernel allows, is the entire opening SmoothQuant walks into.

Why the reduction axis is off limits

One output tile of an INT8 GEMM, and the two places a scale can be applied without breaking the MMA sequence.

A scale on the token axis or the output-channel axis multiplies the accumulator after the reduction finishes — one FP16 multiply per output element. A scale on the input-channel axis would have to land between the MMAs, inside the K-loop, where the Tensor Core pipeline has no room for it. The constraint is about where a number can be applied, not how expensive it is.

Q4 · Goal

INT8 on both operands, in a shape the kernel already runs

No mixed precision, no retraining, no per-channel scale at runtime — the output of the quantized layer should be computed by a plain INT8 GEMM with scales only on the outer dimensions, and the accuracy should hold at 175B.

Q5 · Method — 1/2 · the move

Divide the activations, multiply the weights, offline

Since Y = (X·diag(s)⁻¹)(diag(s)·W) is exactly XW for any positive s, the per-channel scale can be applied before inference and folded into the previous layer's parameters — the runtime kernel never sees it.

This is the whole trick, and it is worth being clear about why it is not cheating. Nothing is thrown away and nothing is approximated: the transform is an identity on the product. What changes is only how the two operands are shaped before they meet the quantizer. Difficulty is being moved, not removed.

Q6 · Method — 2/2 · the knob

Difficulty is conserved, so the only question is where to put it

s_j = max|X_j|α / max|W_j|1−α — at α = 0 the weights are flattened and the activations absorb everything, at α = 1 it is the other way round, and both endpoints quantize badly, so the method is the interior point.

The paper reports α = 0.5 as a good default for OPT and BLOOM, and 0.75 for GLM-130B, whose activations are more extreme. On the two Qwen3-8B layers below the optimum lands at α = 0.4 and 0.6 — near the default, and different between layers, which is the honest version of the story.

α = 0 is not "leave it alone" — it is the first extreme. s_j = 1/max|W_j| flattens the weights perfectly and hands every bit of the difficulty to the activations, which is why the ridge at α = 0 is taller than the untouched number in the sub-label. Push α to the other end and the two panels trade places. Neither surface is ever improved for free: one is paid for with the other, and the figures above the plots say the exchange rate.

Q7 · Validation

Every OPT and BLOOM size, plus GLM-130B and MT-NLG 530B

Seven zero-shot tasks and one language-modelling benchmark, against four INT8 baselines, at three efficiency levels O1–O3, ending with a 530B model served on eight GPUs instead of sixteen.

The O1/O2/O3 ladder is worth noticing: O1 keeps per-token dynamic activation scales, O2 goes per-tensor dynamic, O3 goes per-tensor static — no calibration at runtime at all. The claim is that smoothing makes even the cheapest, least-adaptive setting work, which is a stronger statement than any single accuracy number.

Q8 · Results

The tax is what per-channel would have fixed, and smoothing gets it back

On OPT-175B, plain INT8 per-tensor falls from 71.6% to 32.3% — chance-level — while per-channel would have held 71.4%; SmoothQuant-O3 recovers that without ever using a per-channel scale at runtime.

What the paper measured paper

Their Table 3, charted. We did not re-run OPT-175B — re-running a headline is not understanding.

Three methods of that generation do not merely lose accuracy — they take perplexity from 10.99 into the tens of thousands, which is a model that has stopped being a model. LLM.int8() survives by keeping the outliers in FP16 and paying for a mixed-precision matmul. SmoothQuant survives at O3, the setting with no runtime calibration at all, which is the strongest form of the claim: the tensor was fixed beforehand, so the cheapest possible kernel is enough.

What it buys at the serving end paper

Their Table 8 — OPT-30B decoding on one GPU, batch 1.

Halving the bytes is the point at batch 1, where decoding is memory-bound: the step gets faster and the footprint halves at the same time. That second number is what turns a 175B model from eight GPUs into four, and it is why an accuracy result with no speed result would not have been interesting.

Q9 · Comparison

Against the outlier-handling generation

LLM.int8() keeps outliers in FP16 and pays with a mixed-precision matmul; ZeroQuant and Outlier Suppression stay on legal axes and therefore do not address the channel variance; SmoothQuant changes the tensor instead of the kernel.

The comparison with GPTQ is not a competition — they occupy different regimes. GPTQ is weight-only, W4A16, and computes in FP16; SmoothQuant is W8A8 and computes in INT8. The next page in this track, AWQ, goes back to weight-only, and does it with the same per-channel scale you just watched — aimed at a different target.

Q10 · Significance

It made the difficulty a variable

Before this paper, activation outliers were an obstacle to be detected and routed around; after it, they are a quantity that can be moved between the two operands by a number you choose.

Q11 · Limitations

One α, chosen from outside

α is a hyperparameter set per model family rather than derived — 0.5 for OPT and BLOOM, 0.75 for GLM-130B — and the scales come from a calibration set, so a distribution shift at serving time is not something the method can see.

Our sweep makes the same point at layer granularity: the optimum differs between two layers of the same model. A per-layer search is cheap and obvious in hindsight — and it is exactly the step AWQ takes, replacing the fixed α with a search against the layer's own output error.

Q12 · Future work

The knob generalises; the bit width was the easy part

Once difficulty is a movable quantity, the same transform shows up wherever two operands share a quantization budget — which is how the idea reappears, one year later, in a weight-only method aiming at four bits.
